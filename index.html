Low glucose
If Charlie is below his low threshold, act straight away.
Low = usually below 4.0 mmol/L
Use Charlie's personalised care plan if it gives a different threshold or treatment amount.
What to do now
1
Stop what Charlie is doing
Stay with him. If he is exercising, stop the activity.
2
Give fast-acting carbohydrate
Give the exact amount listed in Charlie's care plan. Examples can include glucose tablets, glucose gel or a small sugary drink.
3
Wait 10–15 minutes
Keep Charlie with you and monitor how he is feeling.
4
Check glucose again
If it is still below 4.0 mmol/L, repeat the hypo treatment and recheck after another 10 minutes.
5
Once recovered
Follow Charlie's care plan for what happens next, including food or his next meal if required.
If Charlie is very unwell
Do not give food or drink
If Charlie is unconscious, having a seizure, very drowsy or unable to swallow safely: put him in the recovery position, give glucagon if it is prescribed and you are trained to use it, and call 999.
Important
The exact hypo treatment amount should be stored in Charlie's care plan. This app should never invent a child's treatment dose.
High glucose
Work through the checks in order. Use Charlie's care plan first.
High glucose
A high reading is not automatically an emergency. What matters is how high it is, whether it is coming down, ketones and how Charlie feels.
What to do now
1
Check the reading and trend
Look at Charlie's CGM and confirm the reading is real if it does not match how he feels.
2
Check ketones when required
Follow Charlie's sick-day / high-glucose plan. NHS guidance recommends checking ketones when glucose is high, particularly if he is unwell.
3
Follow the authorised insulin plan
Use the Omnipod / prescribed insulin system exactly as Charlie's care plan says. Do not calculate a correction dose in this app.
4
Give sugar-free fluids
Encourage Charlie to drink water or another sugar-free fluid, unless his care team has told you otherwise.
5
Keep checking
Continue monitoring glucose and ketones according to Charlie's plan. If the glucose is not coming down, contact his diabetes team / parent.
Ketones: when to escalate
0
Below 0.6 mmol/L
Usually considered normal. Continue following Charlie's high-glucose plan.
!
0.6–1.5 mmol/L
If Charlie is unwell, contact his diabetes team urgently. Follow his personalised ketone instructions.
!
1.6–3.0 mmol/L
Contact the diabetes team urgently, even if Charlie feels well, and follow his sick-day plan.
!
Above 3.0 mmol/L
This can indicate DKA. Call 999 or go to A&E, especially if Charlie has DKA symptoms.
Call 999 / go to A&E now
If Charlie is vomiting, has stomach pain, is breathing faster or deeper than usual, has fruity-smelling breath, is very drowsy or confused, or has very high ketones.
Important
The thresholds shown here are NHS reference guidance. Charlie's personalised diabetes-team plan should take priority if it specifies different actions.
